Chapter ThreeFood Safety 101 — Non-Negotiable
🚨
This chapter can never be skipped. Food safety violations don't just cost the store — they can make customers seriously ill. Every single rule in this chapter is law, not a suggestion.

The Danger Zone — Memorize This

Bacteria multiply fastest between 41°F and 135°F. That temperature range is called the Danger Zone. Your job is to keep food out of it as much as possible.

ZoneTemperatureWhat It Means
❄️ Cold Hold41°F or belowSafe for refrigerated items
⚠️ Danger Zone41°F – 135°FBacteria party — limit time here to 4 hours max
🔥 Hot Hold135°F or aboveSafe for hot foods (soups, rotisserie, etc.)

Personal Hygiene — The Basics

  • Wash hands for at least 20 seconds: before your shift, after handling raw meat, after using the restroom, after breaks, after touching your face.
  • Wear gloves when handling ready-to-eat foods. Change them between tasks.
  • Keep hair tied back and covered with a hat or hairnet.
  • No jewelry past the wrist (rings, bracelets) — bacteria love to hide there.
  • If you're sick (vomiting, diarrhea, jaundice, open wound on hands) — you cannot work food prep. Call your manager before coming in.

Cross-Contamination — How to Avoid It

✓
Separate raw from ready-to-eat

Never cut raw chicken on the same board you used for cheese. Dedicate color-coded cutting boards by food type.

✓
Clean & sanitize between uses

Clean = remove visible food/soil. Sanitize = kill the germs. You need both steps, in that order.

✓
Store items correctly

Raw meat always goes on the bottom shelf, below ready-to-eat items. No exceptions.

Labeling & Dating — FIFO Rule

FIFO = First In, First Out. When restocking, move older product to the front and put newer product behind it. Every item must be labeled with what it is and when it was prepped or opened.

🏷️
Label it or toss it. If a product doesn't have a date label, your manager has the authority to discard it on the spot — and they should. Don't let product go unlabeled.

The 14-Section Food Safety Inspection

Keith's conducts regular formal food safety inspections covering 14 areas of your department. Each section is pass/fail with photo documentation. Know these areas:

Temperature Control Personal Hygiene Cross-Contamination Labeling & Dating Cleaning & Sanitizing Pest Control Equipment Condition Storage Practices Thawing Procedures Cooking Temps Allergen Awareness Waste Disposal Chemical Storage Employee Training Records
⚡ Quick Knowledge Check
  1. What temperature range is the "Danger Zone" for bacteria growth?
  2. How long should you wash your hands?
  3. What does FIFO stand for, and why does it matter?
  4. Where should raw meat always be stored in the cooler?

Answers: (1) 41°F – 135°F  |  (2) 20 seconds minimum  |  (3) First In, First Out — prevents waste and spoilage  |  (4) Bottom shelf, below all ready-to-eat food.

How to Complete a Cleaning Log in Deli Pro

1
Open the App

Go to your store's Deli Pro dashboard and tap "Cleaning Logs" from the navigation menu.

2
Select Your Equipment

Choose the piece of equipment you just cleaned from the list.

3

Mark as Complete, and enter any notes if something was off (broken, damaged, needs a part).

4

Your name and timestamp are auto-logged. This creates an auditable record for inspections.

⚠️
Skipping a log is the same as not doing the task. If a coordinator or inspector asks to see cleaning records and yours aren't there, it's treated as a failure — even if you actually cleaned the equipment. Log it every time.

Temperature Log Expectations

Temps must be recorded in Deli Pro at the start of every shift and again at close. If a unit is out of range:

  • Do not ignore it or hope it fixes itself.
  • Notify your manager immediately.
  • Move product to a safe unit if possible.
  • Document the issue and resolution in Deli Pro's notes field.

Chapter SixOrdering & Receiving Product

Running out of sliced turkey on a busy Saturday is a bad day. Getting a delivery of product you can't verify is a bad week. This chapter covers both ends of the supply chain.

The Ordering Process

1
Check Par Levels

Review current on-hand inventory in Deli Pro vs. your par (minimum stock) levels. Anything below par needs to be ordered.

2
Build the Order

In Deli Pro, select the supplier and add items to your order cart with correct quantities.

3
Manager Review

All orders must be reviewed and approved by your department manager before submission.

4
Submit & Confirm

Submit through Deli Pro. A confirmation number is generated. Save it.

Receiving a Delivery — The Checklist

  • Verify the delivery truck is from an approved Keith's supplier
  • Check the packing invoice — does quantity match what was ordered?
  • Inspect all product for damage, swelling, or off-odors
  • Take temp of refrigerated/frozen items before accepting (cold items must arrive at 41°F or below)
  • Check "best by" or "use by" dates — never accept product already past date
  • Sign the delivery receipt only after verifying the above
  • Immediately put cold product in the cooler — do not leave it sitting
  • Log the received items and any discrepancies in Deli Pro
🚫
Never sign for a delivery you haven't checked. Once you sign, the store is legally accepting responsibility. If something is wrong after the fact, getting credit is a headache. Take the extra 5 minutes upfront.

Chapter SevenCustomer Service Standards

The deli counter is one of the most personal spots in the store. Customers don't just want their cold cuts — they want to feel like they matter. Because they do.

The Keith's Customer Experience — 5 Standards

1
Acknowledge Immediately

If a customer approaches your counter, make eye contact and greet them within 10 seconds — even if you're in the middle of something. "I'll be right with you!" goes a long way.

2
Listen First, Serve Second

Repeat their order back before slicing. "So that's a half-pound of the honey ham, sliced thin — got it!" shows you're paying attention.

3
Offer a Sample

Not sure they'll like the new turkey? Offer a taste. Sales go up. Complaints go down. It's that simple.

4
Handle Complaints Gracefully

When something goes wrong: Listen. Apologize. Fix it. Thank them. Never argue with a customer, and never tell them they're wrong.

5
Send Them Off Right

Say thank you, use their name if you know it, and invite them back. "Enjoy your weekend — hope that turkey hits the spot!"

Phrases That Work — and Phrases to Drop

Say This ✅Not This ❌
"I'd be happy to help you with that!""That's not my department."
"Let me find out for you.""I don't know."
"I'm so sorry about that — let me make it right.""That's not my fault."
"Would you like to try a sample?""We only have what's out."
"Thank you, have a great day!"Silence / no farewell.
⭐
Remember: A customer who has a great experience tells 2–3 people. A customer who has a bad experience tells 10. You are Keith's reputation — every single interaction.

Chapter EightFood Cost & Waste — Why Every Penny Matters

Here's a truth a lot of new employees don't know: thin margins are a grocery store's reality. A deli department typically operates on tight food cost targets. When food is wasted, everyone feels it — from the budget to the paycheck.

What "Food Cost" Means

Food cost is the percentage of your sales revenue that went toward buying the food you sold. If your deli sold $1,000 of product and the ingredients cost $280 to purchase, your food cost is 28%.

📊
Your target food cost is set by your manager. Deli Pro tracks this automatically and flags when you're trending over budget. The coordinator can see every store's food cost from the chain-wide dashboard in real time.

The Top 5 Causes of Waste (and How to Fight Them)

#CauseThe Fix
1Overproduction — making more than you sellUse Deli Pro sales data to see what actually moves, and prep accordingly
2Expired product — not rotating FIFORotate every time you restock, no exceptions
3Improper storage — product spoils fasterCover, label, date, and store at correct temp every time
4Portion creep — slices getting too thickUse the recipe spec, portion scales, and standard slice settings
5Damage during handling — product dropped or mishandledSlow down, handle with care, proper wrapping technique

Markdowns and Donations

Product close to expiration should be brought to the manager's attention for possible markdown pricing or donation. Never throw away product that has a markdown or donation option without manager approval. Many communities count on Keith's food donations.

Chapter NineEmergency Procedures
🔴
Read this chapter carefully and remember it. Most of what's in this manual you can look up when you need it. Not this chapter. In an emergency, you need to already know what to do.

Medical Emergency

  • Call 911 immediately if someone is seriously injured or ill.
  • Notify your manager and the front of store simultaneously.
  • Do not move an injured person unless they are in immediate danger.
  • Know where the first aid kit is in your department — if you don't know, find out today.
  • Know the location of the AED defibrillator in your store.

Fire

  • Pull the fire alarm, call 911, and evacuate — in that order.
  • Know your store's evacuation route and assembly point.
  • Never re-enter a building for product or equipment.
  • Only attempt to use a fire extinguisher on a small, contained fire if you've been trained. Use the P.A.S.S. method: Pull, Aim, Squeeze, Sweep.

Refrigeration Equipment Failure

  • Immediately notify your manager.
  • Record the failure time and ambient temperature in Deli Pro notes.
  • Begin moving perishable product to working units — prioritize highest-risk items (raw meat, dairy, seafood) first.
  • If product has been in the danger zone (41°F–135°F) for more than 4 hours, it must be discarded. When in doubt, throw it out. A health claim is far more expensive than the food.

Customer Injury (Slip, Cut, etc.)

  • Stay with the customer, call for your manager.
  • Do not admit fault or make statements about liability.
  • Complete an incident report with your manager before end of shift.
  • If the floor is wet or hazardous, place warning signs immediately — before doing anything else.

Chapter TenYour First 30 Days — A Roadmap to Success

Everyone has a learning curve. Here's what a successful first month looks like at Keith's Superdeli.

Week 1 — Observe and Ask

  • Shadow your lead or manager for at least 2 full shifts before working solo.
  • Learn where everything is: product, tools, chemicals, safety equipment.
  • Complete your food safety certification if required by your state/store.
  • Get your Deli Pro login and walk through each section with your manager.
  • Ask every question you have — write them down so you don't forget.

Week 2 — Get Your Hands Dirty

  • Handle your first solo temperature logs and cleaning logs.
  • Practice slicing and portioning to spec.
  • Take your first solo customer interactions with a coworker nearby.
  • Participate in a product count or receiving process.

Week 3 — Build Your Rhythm

  • Start your shift without being prompted through each task.
  • Know your top-selling SKUs by heart.
  • Complete a full Deli Pro cleaning log entry independently.
  • Handle a customer complaint successfully and report it to your manager.

Week 4 — Earn Your Stripes

  • Run a full opening or closing without assistance.
  • Review your performance with your manager — get honest feedback.
  • Identify one thing you want to improve in Month 2.
  • Celebrate — you survived your first month! 🎉
